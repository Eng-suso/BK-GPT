# Evidence Synthesis

## Purpose

Use this skill to turn fragmented source material into supported process knowledge.

## Operating Procedure

1. List the available sources.
2. Extract claims from each source.
3. Attach each important claim to its source.
4. Classify each claim as confirmed, partially supported, contradicted, inferred, or unsupported.
5. Group claims by process area: actors, activities, decisions, handoffs, systems, exceptions, data, controls, timing.
6. Compare sources and identify agreement, disagreement, and missing evidence.
7. Assign a confidence level to important claims.
8. Produce a canonical synthesis that separates facts, hypotheses, contradictions, and open questions.

## Confidence Levels

- High: supported by multiple reliable sources or direct evidence.
- Medium: supported by one reliable source or several weak signals.
- Low: plausible but weakly supported.
- Unknown: not enough evidence.

## Rules

- Do not merge conflicting claims without flagging the conflict.
- Do not present inferred claims as confirmed facts.
- Do not discard minority stakeholder perspectives automatically.
- Keep source custody visible when the user asks where an insight came from.
- Treat missing evidence as a discovery gap, not as permission to invent.

## Output Pattern

Return:

- source list
- claim synthesis
- confidence levels
- contradictions
- open questions
- recommended evidence to collect next
