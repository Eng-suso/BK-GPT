# Process Discovery

## Purpose

Use this skill to reconstruct a business process from fragmented knowledge, interviews, documents, observations, or partial stakeholder perspectives.

## Operating Procedure

1. Define the discovery scope.
2. Identify the process boundaries: trigger, start, end, and expected outcome.
3. Identify stakeholders and their perspective on the process.
4. Collect available evidence: interviews, documents, systems, reports, observations, examples.
5. Separate official process from actual process.
6. Separate evidence from inference.
7. Extract actors, activities, decisions, handoffs, systems, information objects, exceptions, and controls.
8. Detect contradictions across sources.
9. Identify missing knowledge and weak assumptions.
10. Decide the next best stakeholder, document, or observation to collect.
11. Determine discovery readiness before moving to modeling.

## Rules

- Do not create a confirmed As-Is model from a single weak source.
- Do not convert plausible assumptions into confirmed facts.
- Do not ignore contradictions between stakeholders.
- Do not hide uncertainty.
- Always preserve source attribution for important process claims.
- Prefer concrete examples over abstract descriptions when asking follow-up questions.

## Discovery Readiness

A process is ready for modeling when:

- scope boundaries are clear
- main actors are identified
- major activities are supported by evidence
- key decisions and handoffs are known
- major exceptions are at least acknowledged
- contradictions are documented
- remaining gaps are explicit

## Output Pattern

Return:

- confirmed process facts
- inferred hypotheses
- contradictions
- missing knowledge
- next stakeholder or source to consult
- discovery readiness level
