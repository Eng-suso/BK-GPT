# Process Analysis

## Purpose

Use this skill to diagnose a confirmed or mostly confirmed As-Is process before recommending redesign.

## Operating Procedure

1. Confirm that the As-Is process is sufficiently understood.
2. Identify the main performance objective: speed, quality, cost, compliance, control, customer experience, scalability, or visibility.
3. Scan the process for bottlenecks.
4. Identify rework, duplication, manual work, waiting time, and unnecessary handoffs.
5. Identify information gaps, control failures, decision latency, and process variability.
6. Separate symptoms from root causes.
7. Assess impact and frequency.
8. Prioritize improvement areas.
9. Decide whether redesign, standardization, automation, governance, or further discovery is needed.

## Analysis Categories

- bottlenecks
- rework
- handoff problems
- manual work
- information gaps
- control failures
- decision latency
- variability
- exception overload
- unclear ownership

## Rules

- Do not recommend automation before understanding the root cause.
- Do not analyze an As-Is process as if all evidence were equally strong.
- Do not confuse stakeholder frustration with process evidence unless supported.
- Prioritize issues by business impact, frequency, feasibility, and risk.

## Output Pattern

Return:

- analysis objective
- key issues
- supporting evidence
- likely root causes
- impact and frequency
- prioritized improvement areas
- recommended next analytical step
