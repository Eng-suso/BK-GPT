# Interview 3 — Francesca Neri, Purchasing Office

**Process:** Purchasing of indirect materials and services
**Date:** 04/09/2026
**Present:** Francesca Neri (Purchasing Office), the consultant

---

**Q. Francesca, you receive the requests from the Technical Office. In what form do they reach you?**

They reach me by email, almost always from Laura. Every now and then something arrives directly from a department and I send it back, because if I start accepting requests from anyone I'll never get out of it. So the informal rule is that there's only one channel, but it's our own rule, it isn't written down anywhere.

**Q. What do you do when the email arrives?**

I read it and check whether it's workable. Workable means I understand what I have to buy and who I can buy it from. If something is missing I ask for it, and that's the bulk of my work on indirect purchases: bouncing requests back to get them completed.

**Q. What percentage of requests get sent back?**

I don't have an exact number because we don't measure it. My feeling is, more than half. Requests for production materials are clean because they have a code in the system. Indirect ones don't, and there it's a job of interpretation.

**Q. Once the request is complete, how do you proceed?**

It depends on the amount and the type of purchase. For small, recurring things I have leeway to go ahead on my own: I issue the order and that's it. Above a certain amount I have to ask my manager for authorization, and in some cases we also need to compare several offers.

**Q. Can you tell me the exact amount and who authorizes?**

I'd rather not give it to you from memory because it's something that has changed and I don't want to give you a wrong number. There's a written internal procedure, I'll get it to you. What I can tell you for sure is that the step exists and that it isn't automatic: it's an email I send and a reply I wait for.

**Q. How long does this wait last?**

It varies a lot. If my manager is in the office, it's a matter of hours. If he's out, it can be days. There's no formal deputy, so if he doesn't reply the file stays stuck. Every now and then I send a reminder.

**Q. How do you choose the supplier?**

For recurring things I have a list of names we've worked with for a long time and that I turn to first. It isn't a formal vendor register, it's my own list built up over time. For new things I ask around for quotes and compare. There's no written rule on how many I have to ask for, I use common sense.

**Q. Let's go back to the urgent requests that come from maintenance. How do you see them?**

I see them afterwards. Most of the time I find out that something has been bought when the invoice arrives, or when Paolo sends me the email with the reference of the order he placed himself. At that point I have to reconstruct the file after the fact: open the order, link it to the supplier, sort out the accounting side.

**Q. So you're the one who does this reconstruction?**

The order part, yes, I do that. The accounting part is done by Administration, but to do it they need me to have opened the order. So let's say the piece that connects things is mine.

**Q. As far as you know, is Paolo aware that you're the one doing it?**

I don't think so, and honestly it's never been said. He sends the email and things get sorted out. We've never sat down to say who does what in that case.

**Q. Is there a point where, in your view, the process breaks down the most?**

At the start. If the request arrived complete the first time, everything else would flow. The second point is the authorization, but that's a problem of people's availability, not of the process.

**Q. And what about visibility for whoever made the request?**

There isn't any. I don't send updates, except when someone asks me for them. It's not ill will: I don't have a tool where I can update a status that others can see, so I'd have to send emails by hand and I don't.

**Q. Last question: what would you change?**

I'd make sure the request is complete from the start, with mandatory fields. And I'd like the authorization to be something you can see, not an email I wait for.
