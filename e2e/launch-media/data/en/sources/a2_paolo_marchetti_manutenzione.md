# Interview 2 — Paolo Marchetti, Head of Maintenance

**Process:** Purchasing of indirect materials and services
**Date:** 03/09/2026
**Present:** Paolo Marchetti (Head of Maintenance), the consultant

---

**Q. Paolo, you're one of the people who start the requests. Walk me through a typical day.**

A typical day starts with a round of the equipment. If during the round I see that something is missing, or that a component needs replacing before it becomes a problem, I make a note of it. At the end of the round I have a list, usually three or four items. Those items become requests.

**Q. How do you send them?**

Email. Always email, to Laura. Once I tried writing directly to Purchasing and they sent it back saying I had to go through the Technical Office, so I stopped. In the email I write what's needed, how many pieces, and by when. If I have the part code I include it, but often I don't have it.

**Q. Why don't you often have it?**

Because the code is on the component's nameplate, and sometimes the nameplate can't be read anymore. Or it's in a manual that's in a cabinet in the workshop. So either I go and look physically, and that's twenty minutes, or I send the request describing the part in words and then someone calls me back to ask for the details. Nine times out of ten it's the second.

**Q. So the email goes out incomplete and then gets completed by phone?**

Yes, that's exactly how it is. It's not a big deal for a single request, it's that it happens every day. Every request brings with it two or three exchanges that shouldn't be there.

**Q. What happens after you've sent the email?**

I don't know. Honestly I don't know, and that's what bothers me most about the process. I send the email and then there's nothing. I don't get a confirmation that the request has been picked up, I don't get an expected date. My point of contact is Laura, so if I want to know something I write to her, but she often has to ask Purchasing too, so it's a long way round.

**Q. And how do the goods reach you?**

They arrive. They arrive at the warehouse and usually the warehouse keeper lets me know, or I notice because I walk past. There's no automatic notification. A couple of times material I'd been waiting two weeks for had arrived and had been sitting in the warehouse for three days without me knowing.

**Q. How do you check that the right thing has arrived?**

I look. I open the box and look to see if it's the right part. If it isn't the right one I tell Laura and the whole round starts again. I don't have a delivery note to sign or a formal procedure, I just look. I have to say we get very few wrong ones, the problem is more the time.

**Q. Tell me about the emergencies.**

Emergencies are a separate chapter. If I have a line down I can't afford to wait for the normal round, so I call the supplier directly, have them send me the part, and then the administrative side gets sorted out afterwards.

**Q. "Sorted out afterwards" by whom?**

I can't tell you for certain. I send the email with what I've ordered and the reference number the supplier gave me, and then someone puts it in order. If you ask me who actually does it, I don't know, and I don't want to give you a random name. I only know that afterwards the thing shows up in the system, so someone has recorded it.

**Q. How often does it happen?**

Not that often, but when it happens it's heavy going. I'd say a few times a month. The point is that when it happens nobody worries about how it went, they only worry about getting the line running again. And so gaps are left behind.

**Q. What kind of gaps?**

Gaps in the sense that if someone later goes to look at what was bought that month, that stuff isn't tracked like the rest. It's not that it disappears, it's that it isn't linked to the original request, because there never was an original request.

**Q. Is there a go-to supplier for these things?**

For mechanical spare parts there are two or three names we use regularly, but I'm not the one who chooses them, and I don't know if there's a contract or an agreement. I call the one I know has the part. If you need to know how they're chosen you'll have to ask Purchasing, I have no visibility on that.

**Q. Last one: what would you change?**

I'd like to know where my request stands without having to ask anyone. And I'd like the part code to be written down once and stay written down, instead of reconstructing it every time.
