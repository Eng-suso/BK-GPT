# Interview 1 — Laura Conti, Head of Technical Office

**Process:** Purchasing of indirect materials and services
**Date:** 02/09/2026
**Present:** Laura Conti (Head of Technical Office), the consultant

---

**Q. Laura, let's start with where the need comes from. Who is the first to notice that something is needed?**

Almost always it's someone on the shop floor. A shift supervisor sees that consumables are running out, or a technician realizes that a scheduled job needs a piece of equipment we don't have. There's no formal moment when this happens: it just happens, usually early in the morning when the day is being planned. Then the request comes to me, and it comes in three different ways depending on who sends it. Some people send me an email, some send me a message, and some stop by my office and tell me in person. In all three cases I end up writing it down again somewhere, because otherwise I forget it.

**Q. So there's no form, nothing structured?**

No. I mean: there is an Excel file that I put together two years ago, but I'm practically the only one who uses it. The others don't fill it in. I stopped insisting because I realized it slowed everything down and people called me anyway. So let's say the file exists as my personal planner, not as a tool of the process.

**Q. When the request reaches you, what exactly do you do?**

The first thing I do is figure out whether I have enough information to move it forward. And that's where it all falls apart, because I almost never do. I get "we need the spare part for the pump" and I don't know which pump, I don't know if it's the one on line two or the one on the chiller unit, I don't know if they've already checked the warehouse. So the round of phone calls begins. Sometimes I sort it out in ten minutes, sometimes it takes me two days because the person who knows the answer is on holiday or out on a site.

**Q. Can you give me a rough idea of how much this part weighs on you?**

Look, if I had to guess, I'd say half of my time on this activity goes into reconstructing what they're asking me for. It's not that I'm deciding anything complicated: I'm just gathering data that someone else already had in their head and didn't write down. And that's the part that makes me angriest, because it's work that serves no purpose.

**Q. And the material references? Codes, data sheets?**

That's another sore point. We don't have a unique internal code for indirect materials. For production materials we do, those are in the system. For indirect ones we don't: everyone uses whatever name comes to mind, or the supplier's catalogue code, or the description from the previous delivery note. So when I need to ask for a quote I have to reconstruct the right reference, often by digging out an old order.

**Q. And once you've reconstructed the request?**

I pass it to Purchasing. I send them an email with what I've managed to put together: description, quantity, when it's needed, and if I have a reference I include it. From then on it's no longer in my hands.

**Q. No longer in your hands meaning you don't know what happens anymore?**

Exactly, and I'm not saying it to complain. I send the email and then I only hear back if someone replies to me, or when the goods arrive. I have no place to look to find out whether they've ordered, whether they're waiting for an offer, whether the supplier has replied. If the department asks me "so, has it arrived?", I have to write to Purchasing and ask.

**Q. Who approves the expense?**

That's a question I can't answer precisely, and I'd rather tell you that than make something up. I don't approve anything. I know that above a certain amount an extra step is needed, but what the amount is and who signs off I can't tell you. Purchasing handles that part and I think it also depends on the type of expense. If you need the details you'll have to ask Francesca.

**Q. Are there cases where the process gets skipped?**

Yes, when there's a real emergency, like the line being down. In that case it barely goes through me at all, they act directly. How exactly it works in those cases I can't really describe because I'm not involved. I know the paperwork gets sorted out afterwards, but I don't know by whom.

**Q. Last one: if you could change just one thing?**

A single place where the request arrives written up properly, with everything that's needed in it. I don't care if it's software or a spreadsheet: I just don't want to have to play archaeologist to understand what they're asking me for.
